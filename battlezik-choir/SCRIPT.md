# BattleZik — "Worthy"

Final script for the choir video (draft 3). **Nothing is built from this until you say go.**

Changes from draft 2: the opponent is now a dragon, and the art direction is hand-painted anime-style 3D, built entirely in code, after the reference video you sent.

- Format: vertical 1080x1920, 60 fps, 2:00 (the full track).
- No voice-over. The choir carries it; the words are on screen.
- Every frame is rendered by code. No photos, no stock footage, no game characters.

## The story in one line

An unknown knight, with no name and no crown, answers a call, climbs to the arena, is burned and beaten down by a dragon, rises, and discovers the power was the knight's own all along.

## What the viewer should feel

- **Worthiness.** Nobody grants it. No king, no birthright, no luck. The knight earns it by getting up, and the film says so in plain words at its turning point. At the end even the dragon lowers its head.
- **Power.** It builds with the music: a small figure at a dying fire, then armed, then striking, then standing inside a storm of blue fire that parts a dragon's flame.
- **That it is about them.** The words speak to "you" throughout. The knight's visor never lifts, so any player can be inside the armour.

BattleZik appears only at the end, as the arena where this happens. No feature is named.

## The characters

- **The Knight.** Plain, battered steel plate. No crest, no colours, a blank shield. A thread of blue light lives in the sword and grows through the film.
- **The Dragon.** Vast, black-scaled, horned, with wings that blot out the sky. Its fire is red-gold, the only warm light in the film until worth is earned. It stands for every stronger rival, and for doubt.
- **The Ember.** A single point of blue light. It starts as the last coal of the knight's fire and ends as the blaze around the sword. It is also the BattleZik glow.

## Art direction: hand-painted anime 3D, built entirely in code

The look follows the reference you sent ("Claude's day off", animated by Opus 5.5): 3D scenes that read like frames of a hand-painted animated film. No Blender and no imported models. Every shape, surface and light is written in code.

What that reference does, and what this film will do the same way:

- **Simple, chunky shapes with character.** Everything is built from clean geometric forms with softened edges. The knight is a stylised figure: an oversized helm, broad blocky pauldrons, a big sword. The dragon is built from bold faceted forms: wedge head, horn spikes, plated neck, sail-like wings.
- **Painted shading, not realism.** Surfaces are flat, rich colour with two or three tones: warm where the light hits, cool violet in the shadow, a bright rim along the edge. Brushy texture on stone, wood and cloth is generated in code.
- **A painted sky.** Towering clouds, a glowing horizon and a low sun, as in the reference. The sky sets the mood of each movement: cold grey dawn, storm on the road, smoke and fire in the arena, a blazing golden sunset at the climax.
- **Lens and light.** Strong depth of field with soft blurred foregrounds, glow around bright things, sun flares, drifting dust and embers. This is most of what makes the reference feel expensive.
- **Colour.** Cool steel-blue and violet for the knight's world. Red-gold for the dragon's fire. Arcane blue, the logo's colour, for the knight's power. True gold appears once, at 1:12, and returns for the name.
- **Camera.** Slow push-ins when the knight is alone, low angles that make the dragon tower, a fast orbit at the climax. Framed tall for the phone screen.

**How this differs from the reference.** The reference is sunny and cosy; this film is dramatic. The technique is the same, the palette and lighting are darker and more heroic, and it opens up into warm golden light only at the climax.

**An honest note on quality.** The reference's hero is a box with legs, which is the simplest possible character. A knight and a dragon have more parts and need more animation, so they are harder to do well in this style. They will look like bold, stylised, hand-painted figures, not detailed realistic ones. The script already suits that: the knight has no face, and the dragon is often seen through smoke and backlight. Before building the film I will render one finished still of the knight and one of the dragon, so you can judge the quality and stop me if it is not good enough.

## Typography in motion

The words live inside the scenes, not on top of them: they are lit by the same fire, they cast shadows, and smoke passes in front of them.

- **Power words** (one or two words on a big hit): blackletter, as forged metal or burning letters, filling the width.
- **Lines** (three to five words): Roman capitals, as if cut into stone.
- **Whispers** (small asides): a fine italic serif.
- Words arrive on the beat and behave like what they say: UP rises, FELL drops and cracks, STRIKE is cut in two.
- One thought per bar (2 seconds). Never more than five words on screen at once.
- All words stay inside the area the apps leave uncovered.

The two fonts are downloaded and installed: Pirata One (blackletter) and Cinzel (Roman capitals), both under the free Open Font License.

The three lone hits and the silences stay completely clean.
